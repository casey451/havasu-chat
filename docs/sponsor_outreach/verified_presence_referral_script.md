# Verified Presence — Referral Ask Script

> **Companion to:** `docs/sponsor_outreach/verified_presence_pitch.md`. Used at the end of a "no" conversation when the merchant has clearly declined but the conversation is cordial. Frames the ask as feedback-seeking, not sales-funnel-building. Makes it easy for the merchant to give one name and move on. ~95 words. Drafted via ChatGPT 2026-05-13.

---

## The script

> "I appreciate you taking the time to talk with me, seriously. And no worries on the no — I'd rather get an honest answer than force something that doesn't make sense for your business.
>
> Can I ask one favor before I head out? Is there another local business owner you respect who'd give me honest feedback on whether this thing feels worth $79/month? Doesn't even need to be a referral intro or anything formal — even just a business name helps.
>
> I'm still early enough that blunt local feedback is honestly more useful to me than a forced sale."

---

## When to use it

- The merchant has said no in some form
- The conversation is still cordial (not hostile)
- You're already at the "end of pitch" moment — don't reopen the sale
- Even one business name is useful; don't push for warm intros

## When NOT to use it

- The merchant said no firmly and is clearly done talking — exit gracefully without the ask
- The conversation went badly — don't extract a referral from a soured conversation
- You've already gotten a "yes, let me think" — leave room for the Day 2 / Day 7 follow-up email sequence to work first (see `verified_presence_followup_emails.md`)
- The merchant is in an unrelated category to your current sales focus — referrals to non-Home-Services merchants are less useful in V1

## Tracking referrals

For the first 5–10 referrals, write down (informally — phone notes or a small spreadsheet):

- Who referred whom
- The original merchant's decline reason
- Whether the referred merchant ended up signing up
- Any patterns ("plumbers refer plumbers" vs "anyone refers anyone")

After ~10 referrals, the patterns are clear enough that you can adapt the ask language to specific decline reasons.
