## §1 60-second cold pitch (door-knock or in-person)

> "Hey, are you the owner or the person who handles marketing around here?
>
> My name's Casey. I live here in Havasu and I built a local directory and chat product called Havasu Chat. It's basically a Lake Havasu-specific search and recommendation tool for locals and visitors. People ask it stuff like 'who's a good plumber near the north side,' 'who does emergency AC work,' or 'who can fix a pool leak.'
>
> I'm not selling ads or promising leads. This first package is just called Verified Presence. It's $79 a month and it gives you a verified listing in the directory, puts you in the chat results when people ask relevant questions, and lets you control your business info yourself instead of hoping random internet data is right.
>
> The reason I'm stopping by is I'm starting with home services because that's what people here ask about most, especially with older homes and seasonal residents.
>
> I know you guys probably get pitched constantly by Yelp and all those companies. This isn't that. I'm local, I'm building it myself, and the product already works today.
>
> Can I show you the directory on my phone for two minutes so you can see what I mean?"

If they're busy:

> "No problem. What's usually a better time for me to swing back by when things slow down a little?"

## §6 Anticipated objections and responses

### "I already have a Google listing"

**Underlying concern:** They think this is redundant.

**Response:** "You absolutely should have a Google listing. I'm not telling anyone to replace that. This is more about being present inside a Havasu-specific local search and recommendation product where people are asking direct local questions. It's additive, not replacement."

**What NOT to say:**
- "Google is dying"
- "AI will replace search"
- "You don't need Google anymore"

### "$79 is a lot for a small shop"

**Underlying concern:** They've wasted money before.

**Response:** "I get it. Especially with how many companies pitch local shops every week. That's why I kept the first package simple and month-to-month. If somebody tries it for a while and doesn't think it's worth it, they can cancel. I'd rather somebody say no than sign up expecting miracles."

**What NOT to say:**
- "It's only $79"
- "That's less than a cup of coffee a day"
- "You can't afford not to"

### "Who actually uses this app?"

**Underlying concern:** They think nobody is there yet.

**Response:** "We're early. I'm upfront about that. But the product is live today and I'm building it specifically around the kind of local searches people already make constantly in Havasu. I'd rather be honest about where we are than pretend this is some giant platform already."

**What NOT to say:**
- Fake user numbers
- Inflated growth claims
- "Everybody in town is using it"

### "I don't trust AI" / "I don't want AI making things up about my business"

**Underlying concern:** Loss of control and inaccurate information.

**Response:** "That's actually one of the reasons I built the verified listing system. If your business is verified, you control the info directly — hours, services, photos, descriptions, contact info. I don't want random outdated data floating around either."

**What NOT to say:**
- "The AI knows everything"
- "The AI handles all of it automatically"
- "It's fully autonomous"

### "Send me information and I'll think about it"

**Underlying concern:** Usually a soft brush-off or they haven't understood it yet.

**Response:** "Happy to send something over. I've just found it clicks a lot faster when people see the demo for about a minute. Would it make sense if I stopped by another day when you've got a little more time?"

**What NOT to say:**
- "So is that a yes?"
- "Can I count on you?"
- Pressure tactics

### "How many leads will this generate?"

**Underlying concern:** ROI uncertainty.

**Response:** "I can't honestly promise a lead number right now. The Verified Presence package is mainly about verified visibility and accurate presence inside the platform. The more aggressive lead-capture side is something I'm still building separately. I'd rather under-promise than make up numbers."

**What NOT to say:**
- Guaranteed calls
- Guaranteed ROI
- "You'll make your money back easily"

### "Can I try it free first?"

**Underlying concern:** Fear of wasting money.

**Response:** "Possibly. `[CASEY: confirm whether free trials are allowed]`. What I usually tell people is the package is month-to-month specifically to keep the risk low. I'm trying to keep the business straightforward from the beginning."

**What NOT to say:**
- Endless discounts
- Negotiating against yourself immediately
- Fake urgency

### "I had a bad experience with Yelp / Angi / HomeAdvisor / Nextdoor"

**Underlying concern:** They assume you're the same model.

**Response:** "I hear that constantly. The biggest difference is I'm local, I'm building this specifically for Havasu, and I'm not selling pay-per-lead or locking people into contracts. I'm trying to build something useful long-term, not squeeze shops for short-term ad spend."

**What NOT to say:**
- Trash-talking competitors too aggressively
- "We're nothing like them" without specifics
- Overpromising transparency

## §7 Disqualifiers — when to walk away

- **They demand guaranteed lead volume.** If somebody immediately wants guaranteed calls, guaranteed jobs, or ROI promises for $79/month, they're probably expecting a different product than what Verified Presence is.
- **They want category exclusivity.** If they expect exclusive placement rights or ownership over an entire category at this price point, it's not a fit.
- **They become openly hostile or disrespectful.** Some owners are burned out from constant sales calls. If the conversation turns hostile quickly, move on professionally.
- **They refuse to look at the demo for even 30 seconds.** The product makes more sense visually. If they won't even glance at it, the odds of converting later are low.

## §8 Pre-call checklist (operator-facing)

Before any pitch:

- Pull up their Google Business listing on a separate tab or device.
- Have Havasu Chat open and working on your phone (not loading).
- Have one strong category page ready to demo (Home Services / sub-trade most relevant to the merchant).
- Carry business cards and a simple one-page leave-behind.
- Be ready to show the merchant claim/edit flow from the business-owner side (not just the consumer view).
